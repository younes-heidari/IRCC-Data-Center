Three-Loop Architecture
  • Three loops in series: chilled water → R-290 → glycol → air
  • The intermediate 30% propylene-glycol loop keeps flammable A3 propane out of the data hall
  • A waterside economizer bypasses the compressor entirely when the outdoor air is cold enough
  • Heat is finally rejected at an outdoor dry cooler with variable-speed fans
IRCC 2026 · Project #3 — R-290 Data-Center Chiller

Working-Fluid Selection
  • Screened low-GWP A2L and A3 candidates against a subcritical cycle
  • R-290 (propane) selected: competitive COP with GWP < 3
  • R-1234ze(E) shows a marginally higher COP but needs a meaningfully larger compressor
  • R-290 also fits the existing Bitzer / Kelvion component ecosystem
IRCC 2026 · Project #3 — R-290 Data-Center Chiller
3
Plate Heat-Exchanger Selection
  • Kelvion GBH-DW 500H double-wall BPHE for both evaporator and condenser
  • Double-wall construction gives an A3-safe refrigerant-to-water barrier
  • Thermal sizing by LMTD; refrigerant-side ΔP from the Han (2003) correlation
  • 95-plate margined estimate sits within the 100-plate model limit

The Design Cycle
  • R-290 vapor-compression cycle at 150 kW / 35 °C design ambient
  • Four state points anchor the model: to = 10 °C, tc = 45 °C
  • Design-point COP 4.67 at 15 °C leaving chilled water
  • All properties from CoolProp; consistent across every downstream chapter
IRCC 2026 · Project #3 — R-290 Data-Center Chiller
6
Why the Dry Cooler Is Large
  • The 10 K condensing approach is three stacked steps in series
  • 3 K dry cooler + 4 K glycol rise + 3 K condenser
  • Condensing temperature tc is the single biggest lever on full-load COP
  • Each kelvin is bought with heat-transfer area — the price of the glycol loop

COP Sensitivity
  • Full-load COP falls steadily as condensing temperature rises
  • This is why the dry cooler is sized as large as it is
  • It also explains the thin ≈1% margin at the strict AHRI rating point
  • Evaporator approach (5 K) sets to; the rest is condenser-driven

Free Cooling — The Payoff
  • Below 4 °C outdoor air the economizer carries the full load with no compressor
  • Across the ASHRAE 90.1 economizer band, compressor power collapses
  • The glycol loop that costs COP at the design point pays back every winter hour
  • Load split shifts continuously from mechanical to free cooling

Champaign Climate
